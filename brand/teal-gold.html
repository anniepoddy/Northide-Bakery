01 · The idea

The building is the brand.

For 30 years people have found us by the teal wall and the gold letters. The brand should look like the place you walk into. The trick is keeping those bright, man-made colors from making the food look anything but delicious.

  • Teal frames, food fills.

    Teal is the shopfront: bars, bands, borders and backgrounds for words. The food always sits on Flour or Deep Teal, never on bright teal.

  • Gold is the glaze.

    Gold is used the way glaze is: a thin, shiny layer on top. Buttons, prices, a highlighted word. Under a tenth of any layout, never a whole page.

  • Ink keeps it grown-up.

    Deep Teal, which is close to black, does the heavy lifting for text, the logo and the drawings. That's what makes the bright colors look deliberate instead of loud.

03 · Keeping it yummy

Bright colors, warm food.

Teal sits opposite golden-brown on the color wheel, so a little teal makes crusts and glazes look richer. Too much, sitting right against the food, makes it look cold. These rules keep it on the right side.

  • Food touches Flour or Deep Teal.Bright teal belongs at least one step away, as a frame, band or bar.
  • Never tint the food.No teal or gold filters, overlays or duotones on photos. Keep the light warm and natural.
  • Pick photos with golden-brown in them.Crusts, glazes, fried onions and toasted tops look best against teal. Pale or green foods go on Flour.
  • Gold stays under a tenth.Buttons, prices, stickers, one highlighted word. If everything is gold, nothing looks special.
  • Flat color, no gradients.Solid blocks, like painted signage. No glows, shadows on text or shine effects.
  • Paper texture is fine.A faint paper grain on Flour keeps it feeling like a bag, not a screen.

08 · Quick rules

Keep it sharp.

Do

  • Use big flat blocks of color, like painted signs.
  • Keep most of every surface in Flour or Deep Teal.
  • Keep gold to small touches: buttons, prices, one word.
  • Put food photos on Flour or Deep Teal, in warm light.
  • Draw in one color only, with plenty of space around.

Don't

  • Put Flour or Gold text on bright Teal.
  • Use gradients, glows or drop shadows on text.
  • Tint, filter or duotone the food.
  • Bring back the old red next to the teal. It reads as Christmas.
  • Crowd a layout with drawings, stickers and badges at once.
