# Northside Bakery brand guide

**Brooklyn retail store, 149 N 8th St, Williamsburg. Since 1992.**

A visual preview of this guide is in `brand.html` (open it in a browser).

---

## 1. Who we are

A family-run Polish bakery in Williamsburg since 1992. Pastries and hot food are made in the shop every day, next to a market of European groceries. Rooted in Polish tradition, open to the whole neighborhood.

**Feel:** rustic but modern, warm, bright and delicious. Classy, but never stiff.
**Not:** kitschy, generic farmhouse, cluttered, or like a chain.

---

## 2. Tagline

**Primary:** Baked on North 8th since 1992.

**Alternates:**
- **Smacznego from North 8th.** A playful second line for bags, boxes, stickers and social media. *Smacznego* means "enjoy your meal."
- **A little bit of Poland on North 8th.** For heritage moments: the Our Story section, holiday campaigns, press.

Use one line per surface. Don't stack two taglines together.

---

## 3. Brand pillars

1. **Rodzina: family-made since 1992.** Started by Richard and Teresa Podedworny and still run by the family. Every recipe has a person behind it.
2. **Tradycja: Polish at heart, Brooklyn by address.** Pączki, pierogi and gołąbki are the soul of the shop, and the menu has grown to welcome everyone in the neighborhood.
3. **Świeżość: made fresh, every day.** Pastries and hot food are made in the shop daily. What's on the counter changes with the day and the season.

## 4. Colors

Two colors carry the brand: **red and cream**. Everything else is a lighter or darker step of those two, plus a warm near-black for text.

| Name | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Northside Red | `#9A1B1F` | Headlines, buttons, illustrations, the logo |
| Deep Red | `#741417` | Hover and pressed states, small accents on red |
| Cream | `#F3E6CF` | Main background |
| Paper | `#FBF6EC` | Cards, panels, light sections |
| Ink | `#2B1D1E` | Body text |

**Balance:** about 60% cream and paper, 30% red, 10% ink.

**Contrast (all pass WCAG AA for text):**
- Red on Cream: 6.7:1
- Ink on Cream: 13:1
- Paper on Red: 7.6:1
- Cream on Red: 6.7:1

**Don't:**
- Add other brand colors (no teal, gold, green or pastels). The storefront's teal belongs to the building, not the brand.
- Use gradients.
- Use pure black. Use Ink.

## 5. Typography

| Role | Font | Settings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display (headlines, motto, dish names) | **Fraunces** (Soft 50), SemiBold (600); Italic Medium (500) for the motto and dish names | Tracking -0.01 to -0.02em, line height 0.95 to 1.05 |
| Body (reading text) | **EB Garamond Regular (400)** | 20px on the web (Garamond runs small), line height 1.55, old-style numbers |
| Menus, buttons, labels | **EB Garamond SemiBold, small capitals** | Letter spacing +0.12 to +0.14em, e.g. MENU, ORDER ON GRUBHUB, EVERY DAY |
| Polish names | **EB Garamond Italic**, in Red, smaller, always under the English name | e.g. *Chleb żytni* under "Rye bread", *Pączki* under "Polish doughnuts" |

Both fonts are free from Google Fonts and handle every Polish letter (ą ć ę ł ń ó ś ź ż).

**Why these:** Fraunces is a soft, warm old-style serif that feels hand-made, like the ink drawings and the paper bag; its italic gives the motto and dish names a friendly, confident voice. EB Garamond is a classic book face, warm and easy to read, and set in spaced small capitals it makes menus and buttons feel like an engraved shop sign rather than an app.

**Rules:**
- Sentence case for headlines and text. Labels, menus and buttons use spaced small capitals, never shouty full-size capitals.
- Don't type words in a script font to imitate the logo. The script lives only in the logo.
- At most two weights in one block of text.

## 6. Logo

The wheat-and-script roundel ("Northside BAKERY EST. 1992") is the only logo. Keep it exactly as drawn.

**Approved versions** (in `images/logo/`):
- Red on Cream or Paper: the default
- Cream on Red: footers, bags, stickers
- Ink on Paper: one-color print, receipts

**Rules:**
- **Clear space:** leave at least the height of the "B" in BAKERY on every side.
- **Minimum size:** 120px wide on screen, 1 inch in print.
- Don't stretch, rotate, outline, add shadows or recolor it outside the three versions.
- Don't put it on busy photos.

**To do:** the current file is a small screen-only version. Find the original vector file (.ai, .eps, .svg or .pdf) for print, signage and packaging.

**Heritage:** the original poster (a girl in Polish folk dress holding a loaf) is a heritage image, not a logo. Use it for the story, anniversaries and "since 1992" moments.

## 7. Illustration style

Based on the ink drawings on our current paper bags: bakery objects drawn like old engravings.

**The look:**
- **One color only:** Red on Cream or Paper, or Cream on Red.
- **Hand-drawn ink line** with a slight wobble. One main line weight, plus a thinner line for detail.
- **Shading with short parallel hatch marks** on one side (the shadow side), never filled shapes.
- **Flour-dust dots** (stippling) for texture.
- **Real objects, simply drawn:** loaves, pierogi, pączki, croissants, pretzels, gołąbki, wheat, whisks, rolling pins.
- **No full-color art.**

**Characters** (the playful side, like the mascots in our references):
- The food itself comes to life: **Chlebek** (a loaf), **Pierożek** (a pierogi) and **Pączek**.
- Same ink line, hatching and dots as the objects, plus two dot eyes, a small smile, hatch-mark blush, stick arms and legs.
- They walk, wave and steam. One or two per surface, never a crowd.
- Use them for moments of delight: next to headings, in the footer, on stickers and boxes. The objects stay the workhorse for borders and patterns.
- Files: `chlebek-character.svg`, `pierozek-character.svg`, `paczek-character.svg`.
- No sparkles, stars or other decorative filler: they read as gimmicky.

**How to use them:**
- **Borders and bands,** like the band along the bottom of the bag: a row of objects, slightly rotated (±10°), evenly spaced.
- **Scattered at the edges** of a page, box or bag, never in the middle of the content.
- **Placeholders** for menu items without a photo yet.
- **Never on top of** food photos, the logo or text.
- **Minimum size:** 32px on screen, 0.5 inch in print.

**Current library** (`images/doodles/`): `boule`, `baguette`, `pierogi`, `croissant`, `pretzel`, `wheat`, `whisk`, `rolling-pin`, `paczek`, `golabek`, `loaf-oval`, `toast`, `egg`, `rosemary`, `seeds`, `board`, `makowiec`, `babka`, `kielbasa`, and the three characters.

**The bag border:** like our paper bag, drawings can crowd the edges of a surface, overlapping and running off the edge, with the logo calm in the middle. The website hero uses this layout.

**Next to draw:** makowiec (poppy seed roll), a jar of pickles, a kielbasa ring, a mazurek, and the storefront as a line drawing.

## 8. Photography

- Real photos of our food, our shelves and our shop. No stock photos.
- Close and generous: fill the frame with the food, like racks of loaves or trays of pączki.
- **Cutouts:** for hero spreads, packaging and social posts, cut whole items out of their background (`tools/cutout.mjs`) and place them on Cream or Red with a soft shadow. Only use items that are fully in frame.
- Show photos uncropped where possible (they're 3:4), so the whole item is visible.
- Warm, natural light. Avoid harsh flash and blue fluorescent casts.
- People only with their permission.

## 9. Tone of voice

**Like a regular behind the counter:** warm, plain-spoken, a little proud, never pushy.

**Principles:**
1. **Say what it is.** "Cabbage rolls stuffed with rice and meat", not "an elevated take on a classic".
2. **English first, Polish second.** Lead with the English name most customers can read ("Stuffed cabbage rolls") and put the Polish name under it, smaller and in italic (*gołąbki*). Words customers already know, like pierogi and kielbasa, can stand alone. Always use the correct Polish letters (ł, ą, ż…).
3. **Short sentences.** Most things fit on a chalkboard.
4. **Only say what's true.** No invented awards, reviews or "best in town" claims beyond the real ones.
5. **Calm and confident.** One exclamation point per page, at most.
